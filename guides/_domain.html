Getting your web address

This takes about ten minutes and costs roughly £4 a year. You only ever do it once. Follow the steps in order and don't skip step 7 — it's the one that catches everybody out.

Why you buy it and not me. The web address is registered in your name, on your own card, in your own account. That means it is legally yours and nobody can take it away from you — not me, not anyone. If you ever wanted somebody else to look after your website, you could take the address with you.

Buying the address

  1. Go to dash.cloudflare.com Cloudflare are a big, well-known company and they sell web addresses at cost price, which is why it's about £4 rather than the £15 or £20 most places charge.
  2. Create a free account Your email address and a password. They'll send you an email to confirm it — click the link in that email before carrying on.
  3. Click "Domain Registration", then "Register Domain" It's in the menu down the left-hand side.
  4. Type in the address you want and press search I'll have told you which one to look for. If it says it's taken, stop and email me — I'll find you another one.
  5. Add it to the basket and pay It'll be around $5 — they charge in US dollars, so expect about £4 on your statement. Tick the box for auto-renew so it can't lapse by accident.

Pointing it at your website

The address is now yours, but it doesn't know where your website lives yet. These next steps tell it. There are five lines to add and they're nearly identical.

  1. Click your new address, then click "DNS" in the left-hand menu You'll see a mostly empty list and an "Add record" button.
  2. Add these five records Click "Add record", fill in the three boxes, save, and repeat. The first four are the same except for the last number.
    TypeNameContent / Target
    A@185.199.108.153
    A@185.199.109.153
    A@185.199.110.153
    A@185.199.111.153
    CNAMEwwwejpc.github.io
⚠ The bit everyone gets wrong.

Next to each record there's a little cloud icon. It starts orange. You need to click it so it turns grey — grey says "DNS only".

If any cloud is left orange, your website won't get its security padlock and visitors may see a warning. All five must be grey. It's one click each.

  1. Email me to say it's done That's your part finished. I'll do the rest at my end, and your website will be live on your own address, usually within a few hours.
